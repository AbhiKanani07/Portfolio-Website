Bravox Credit

Honest approval odds for 27 credit cards, each judged against the credit bureau its issuer typically pulls.

Open bravoxcredit.com Bravox Vector waitlist

Problem

Most credit tools exist to sell you cards, not tell you the truth. From the outside, approval feels random: issuers don't publish cutoffs, they check different credit bureaus, and your scores can differ between those bureaus. I wanted a tool that estimates real odds and shows its reasoning, including when a card is out of reach.

What I built

  • A rules-based odds engine across 27 cards and 3 bureaus, with plain-language reasons on every result.
  • Per-bureau logic: each issuer is tagged with the bureau it typically pulls, and its cards are judged against that score.
  • A full product around it: explore and filter, 27 card pages, a soft check, a dashboard, accounts, and free tools like a 5/24 counter and a utilization calculator.
  • Security and compliance before launch: PBKDF2 hashing, rate limiting, login lockout, CSP and HSTS, a privacy policy, terms, disclosures, and an FTC affiliate disclosure.

How the odds work

Tiers gate, points score. A card's minimum score tier, utilization ceiling, and inquiry ceiling decide eligibility. Clearing them earns a margin score: 50% score headroom over the tier floor (full credit at 60 points above it), 30% utilization headroom, and 20% inquiry headroom, mapped onto 45 to 96%.

Why 96%, not 100%

Issuers also weigh income, debt-to-income, account age, and internal risk models the engine can't see. Promising near-certainty would be dishonest, so the ceiling stays at 96%. The 45% floor exists because clearing every threshold still isn't a coin flip.

Try the scoring math

This runs the same scoring approach as the production engine, in your browser. The six card archetypes and their thresholds are illustrative, not Bravox's live card data. For real odds, run a check on bravoxcredit.com.

Decisions

Fit is its own score
Approval odds answer "will they say yes?" Fit answers "is this the right card?" Each tier a card sits below your profile costs 12% of its score, floored at 30%, so a near-certain secured card doesn't top an 810 profile's list.
No invented cutoffs
Thresholds come from community-reported data and are labeled as estimates. Per-bureau scores reuse each card's thresholds instead of inventing new ones.
Rebuilt in Next.js
After feedback that the first version looked generic, I rebuilt the frontend as a separate Next.js app, kept the FastAPI backend, and passed on a hybrid and on Kubernetes as over-engineering for this stage.
Not monetized yet
Apply buttons go to official issuer pages. Usefulness and search traffic come first.
Calibration is next
Users can report whether they were approved or denied. Those outcomes are the path from rules to evidence.

YouTube recommendation bias

A graph model of my own watch history, built to measure how the feed steers what I watch next.

View on GitHub

Why

I ran a YouTube channel growing up, so I've thought about recommendations and retention from the creator side for years. This project flips it around: measure how the algorithm nudges my own behavior.

Data and parsing

My watch-history export (JSON and HTML) supplies timestamps, titles, channels, and inferred categories. Pandas pipelines clean and categorize it, then enrich sessions with watch time and return-visit flags.

Analyses

  • Watch time per category and channel return rates
  • Topic transitions: what I watch after each category or channel
  • Preference drift over the years
  • Binge versus casual sessions

The model

Videos and categories become nodes and transitions become edges. The graph estimates likely-next probabilities and simulates what the feed might surface next given recent views. Topic drift and binge likelihood turn the influence into numbers.

Try the transition graph

Illustrative categories and probabilities, not my watch history. Pick a starting topic, raise the reinforcement to see how a feed that repeats the current topic narrows a session, then simulate one.

Sleep insights dashboard

A pipeline for my Fitbit and Apple Health exports, built to make a lifelong sleep problem measurable.

View on GitHub

Why

I've had sleep issues for as long as I can remember, so I turned them into a pipeline: clean, combine, analyze, present. The data supports decisions, but it never tells the whole story.

Ingestion

  • Fitbit and Apple Health exports: sleep stages (light, REM, deep), overnight heart rate, and HRV
  • Bedtime and wake-time consistency, screen-time proxies, and tags for interventions like a new pillow or evening workouts
  • Synthetic data that mirrors the Fitbit format, for safe demos

Analysis

  • Duration versus bedtime consistency and evening screen time
  • HRV and overnight heart-rate drift as recovery and stress signals
  • Bad-night effects on the next day's steps, focus and mood proxies, and workouts
  • Weekday versus weekend patterns

Modeling

  • Classification: good versus bad nights
  • Regression: total sleep duration from behavior signals
  • Anomaly detection: unusual nights flagged for follow-up, not diagnosis

Try it on synthetic nights

Twelve weeks of synthetic nights shaped like a Fitbit export, the same approach the project uses for safe demos. Change the habits, then change how unusual a night has to be before it's flagged.

Dutch water quality

Seasonal and regional analysis of water measurements across the Netherlands, with anomaly checks that flag stations for follow-up.

Open the live project

Problem

Water boards and utilities have more measurements than time. The questions are where and when to sample next, and which readings deserve a rapid follow-up test.

Approach

  • Merged multi-region datasets, then cleaned and standardized fields with NumPy and Pandas
  • Engineered seasonal and location features and ran variance and trend analysis by province and month
  • Built anomaly checks that surface stations for rapid follow-up testing

Who it helps

  • Water boards and utilities prioritizing sampling routes by province and month
  • Pet owners, aquaculture, and recreation groups checking whether conditions are acceptable
  • Anyone who needs exportable summaries and clear visuals for reporting

Tools

Python with NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn in Jupyter, plus data-quality checks, anomaly heuristics, and presentation-ready charts.

Illustrative values for 12 provinces over 12 months. In this demo, each reading is compared with what its province and month would predict, and anything past the threshold is flagged. The live project has the real analysis.
