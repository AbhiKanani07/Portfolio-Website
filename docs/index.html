      Bravox Credit

      Honest approval odds for 27 credit cards, each judged against the credit bureau its issuer typically pulls.

      Open bravoxcredit.com Bravox Vector waitlist

      Problem

      Most credit tools exist to sell you cards, not tell you the truth. From the outside, approval feels random: issuers don't publish cutoffs, they check different credit bureaus, and your scores can differ between those bureaus. I wanted a tool that estimates real odds and shows its reasoning, including when a card is out of reach.

      What I built

      • A rules-based odds engine across 27 cards and 3 bureaus, with plain-language reasons on every result.
      • Per-bureau logic: each issuer is tagged with the bureau it typically pulls, and its cards are judged against that score.
      • A full product around it: explore and filter, 27 card pages, a soft check, a dashboard, accounts, and free tools like a 5/24 counter and a utilization calculator.
      • Deployed solo to production with secure auth (PBKDF2, login lockout), rate limiting, and CSP and HSTS headers, plus a privacy policy, terms, disclosures, and an FTC affiliate disclosure.

      How the odds work

      Tiers gate, points score. A card's minimum score tier, utilization ceiling, and inquiry ceiling decide eligibility. Clearing them earns a margin score: 50% score headroom over the tier floor (full credit at 60 points above it), 30% utilization headroom, and 20% inquiry headroom, mapped onto 45 to 96%.

      Why 96%, not 100%

      Issuers also weigh income, debt-to-income, account age, and internal risk models the engine can't see. Promising near-certainty would be dishonest, so the ceiling stays at 96%. The 45% floor exists because clearing every threshold still isn't a coin flip.

      Try the scoring math

      This runs the same scoring approach as the production engine, in your browser. The six card archetypes and their thresholds are illustrative, not Bravox's live card data. For real odds, run a check on bravoxcredit.com.

      Decisions

      Fit is its own score
      Approval odds answer "will they say yes?" Fit answers "is this the right card?" Each tier a card sits below your profile costs 12% of its score, floored at 30%, so a near-certain secured card doesn't top an 810 profile's list.
      No invented cutoffs
      Thresholds come from community-reported data and are labeled as estimates. Per-bureau scores reuse each card's thresholds instead of inventing new ones.
      Rebuilt in Next.js
      After feedback that the first version looked generic, I rebuilt the frontend as a separate Next.js app, kept the FastAPI backend, and passed on a hybrid and on Kubernetes as over-engineering for this stage.
      Not monetized yet
      Apply buttons go to official issuer pages. Usefulness and search traffic come first.
      Calibration is next
      Users can report whether they were approved or denied. Those outcomes are the path from rules to evidence.

      Keystroke

      An iOS app that turns any image into a custom Apple Wallet pass.

      What it does

      Pick an image and Keystroke turns it into a custom pass for Apple Wallet. The SwiftUI app sends the image to a FastAPI backend over a REST API, and the backend processes the upload and builds a pass that meets Wallet's signing requirements.

      Why signing is the hard part

      Wallet only accepts signed passes. A pass is a bundle of files, such as pass.json and its images, plus a manifest that lists a SHA-1 hash of every file and a signature over that manifest made with an Apple-issued certificate. Change one byte of any file and Wallet rejects the pass.

      The app

      Swift and SwiftUI on the phone: choose an image, preview the pass, and hand off to the backend.

      The backend

      Python and FastAPI on the server: process the image upload, assemble the pass bundle, and handle Apple Wallet's pass-signing requirements.

      Build a pass bundle

      This runs in your browser. Pick an image or use your own, which never leaves this page. The pass colors come from the image, and every file in the bundle is hashed with real SHA-1 into manifest.json. The final step, the signature, needs an Apple-issued certificate, so here it stays a placeholder.

      Answer engine

      The assistant on this page answers questions about Abhi from a published record, cites every answer, and says so when something isn't in the record.

      Open the Sandbox

      What it does

      Visitors ask in plain English. The engine fixes typos against the record's own vocabulary, answers multi-part questions part by part, and declines private topics, internal Quest details, and anything the record can't support.

      How it ranks

      BM25 keyword search with synonyms and intent boosts, blended with an intent classifier and LSA embeddings trained on the record. The blend weights were tuned on questions held out of training.

      Rules come first

      Privacy, prompt injection, and internal details are decided by rules before any model runs, so a model can never talk its way past them.

      No made-up facts

      Answers are composed only from entries in the record, and every one cites its source. When the best match is too weak, the answer says it isn't in the record.

      Probe the models

      Type a question to see what the trained models think, live: the intent classifier's top five entries by probability and the five nearest entries by meaning. The highlighted entry is the one the full engine answers from.
